Assumptions & statutory sources (verified Sep 2026)

  • Formula: OT pay = OT hours × hourly rate × multiplier. Hourly rate from monthly salary = salary ÷ (26 days × 8 hours) = ÷ 208 (stated assumption; edit the hourly rate directly if your payroll uses another divisor).
  • 2× statutory: Factories Act 1948 §59 — overtime beyond 9 hrs/day or 48 hrs/week at twice the ordinary rate (basic + allowances). OSH Code 2020 §27 also prescribes 2×.
  • Shops & Establishments Acts: Maharashtra (2017 Act §15), Karnataka (§7), Delhi rules — all mandate 2× the ordinary/normal rate for overtime. Limits vary (e.g. Delhi: 150 hrs/year; Maharashtra: 144 hrs/quarter after 2026 amendments).
  • 1.5×/1× are not statutory minima — use them only where a contract, standing order or company policy sets a different rate for non-statutory overtime.
  • Sources: LiveLaw — SC on §59 overtime wages; Mondaq — overtime under S&E Acts; ET — overtime under new labour codes

Overtime calculator — FAQs

What is the legal overtime rate in India?

Under the Factories Act 1948 (§59) and the Shops & Establishments Acts of most states (Maharashtra, Karnataka, Delhi), overtime beyond the statutory daily/weekly limits must be paid at twice the ordinary rate of wages. The OSH Code 2020 (§27) keeps the same 2× rule.

भारत में ओवरटाइम की दर क्या है?

फ़ैक्टरीज़ एक्ट 1948 (धारा 59) और अधिकांश राज्यों के Shops Acts के तहत तय सीमा से ज़्यादा काम पर सामान्य मज़दूरी की दोगुनी दर से ओवरटाइम मिलता है।

How is the hourly rate derived from monthly salary?

Monthly salary ÷ 208 (26 working days × 8 hours). This is a common payroll convention, not a statutory formula — enter your own hourly rate if your company uses a different divisor.

When would I use 1.5× or 1×?

Only where a contract, certified standing order or company policy sets a different rate for overtime that is not triggered by the statutory limits — the statutory minimum stays 2×.
