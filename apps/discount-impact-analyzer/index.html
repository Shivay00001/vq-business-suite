Required sales uplift = d ÷ (m − d), where d = discount % and m = margin % on price

Assumptions stated on-screen

  • Margin is on selling price — (price − cost) ÷ price. The d ÷ (m − d) formula requires this definition.
  • Fixed costs unchanged by the discount. If the sale needs extra ad spend or staff, add it to unit cost first.
  • Unit cost stays constant at higher volume (no bulk discounts modelled).
  • Loss-making flag: discount ≥ margin means every discounted unit loses money — no volume recovers it.

Discount impact analyzer — FAQs

How much extra sales do I need after giving a discount?

Required uplift = discount ÷ (margin − discount). A 10% discount at 40% margin needs 10 ÷ (40 − 10) = 33.3% more units to earn the same total profit.

छूट देने के बाद कितनी ज़्यादा बिक्री चाहिए?

ज़रूरी बढ़ोतरी = छूट ÷ (मार्जिन − छूट)। 40% मार्जिन पर 10% छूट के लिए 33.3% ज़्यादा यूनिट बेचनी होंगी ताकि कुल मुनाफ़ा बराबर रहे।

When does a discount become unrecoverable?

When the discount % equals or exceeds your margin %. Then every discounted unit loses money and no volume can fix it — the only options are a smaller discount or a lower cost.

Does this account for fixed costs?

The formula uses contribution margin per unit, so it holds as long as fixed costs do not change with the discount. If the discount needs extra staff or ad spend, add that to your unit cost first.

Is this margin on price or on cost?

Margin on selling price: (price − cost) ÷ price. The d ÷ (m − d) formula requires margin measured on price, which is the standard retail definition.
